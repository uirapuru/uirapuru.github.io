Privacy Policy — SniperTAK

Last updated: 18 November 2026 (version 0.11.1)

SniperTAK is a plugin for ATAK-CIV developed by Grzegorz Kaszuba ("the developer").

The plugin computes ballistic firing solutions and draws observation post, target, and range and bearing items on the ATAK map. It can read weather data from a Kestrel 5700 meter and distance from a Vortex Impact 4000 rangefinder, send corrections to a Garmin watch, and take voice commands.

Data the plugin collects

None. The plugin does not send any personal or device data to the developer or to any third party.

Specific facts behind that statement:

  • The plugin declares no Android permissions of its own. It works with the permissions already granted to ATAK-CIV.
  • The plugin contains no networking code. It opens no internet connections.
  • The plugin contains no analytics, advertising, or tracking libraries.
  • The plugin does not use the advertising identifier.
  • The plugin has no user account, no login, and never asks for an email address.

Kestrel and Vortex devices

You may pair the plugin with a Kestrel 5700 weather meter or a Vortex Impact 4000 rangefinder. The connection uses Bluetooth Low Energy. The plugin reads measurements from the device — wind, temperature, humidity, air density, distance — and uses them inside the plugin, on the same phone or tablet. Nothing from these devices leaves the phone or tablet through the plugin.

Garmin watch

The watch link is off by default. When you turn it on in the settings, the plugin sends to the SniperTAK app on your Garmin watch the data the watch displays: the target name, the range, and the elevation and windage corrections. Nothing else is sent to the watch.

The data goes from the phone to the watch in one of two ways: through the Garmin Connect app on the same phone, or directly over Bluetooth Low Energy, in which case the plugin offers a Bluetooth service that only the watch connects to. When the Garmin Connect app carries the data, Garmin handles it under its own terms.

Voice commands

Voice commands are off by default. When you turn them on, the plugin listens to the microphone through the microphone permission granted to ATAK-CIV and recognises a fixed set of commands, such as switching the target. Recognition runs entirely on the device, with a speech model that you copy to the phone yourself. The plugin does not record, store, or send audio. Ordinary speech that is not one of the commands is ignored.

Files the plugin creates

The plugin writes the following data to storage on the device:

  • Settings — language, units, dialog options, watch and voice switches — in the ATAK shared preferences.
  • Rifle profiles and targets, in ATAK storage on the device.
  • The shot log, in the ATAK application files directory, in snipertak/dziennik.jsonl.
  • Files you export yourself, in the location you pick in the Android save dialog, or in the ATAK documents directory when that dialog is unavailable.

These files stay on the device. The plugin does not upload them. You may delete them at any time through ATAK or a file manager.

Data handled by ATAK

SniperTAK runs inside ATAK-CIV. Markers and lines the plugin places on the map are ATAK map items. Whether they leave the device depends on how you configure ATAK — for example on the TAK server you connect to and the contacts you share data with. That handling is governed by ATAK-CIV and by the servers you configure there. This policy covers only SniperTAK.

Distribution through Google Play

Google Play collects installation and crash data on its own terms, independently of the plugin. See the Google Privacy Policy.

Children

The plugin is intended for users aged 18 or over. It is not directed at children.
